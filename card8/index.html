Vote counts after incarceration

Carl Votes

Came home in 2022. Re-registered on his way out. Has voted in every election since — and he’s telling his brother the rules have changed.

What changed in 2021 — and the step most people miss.

THE SITUATION

Carl is 54. He came home from a New York state prison in 2022 and now lives in Harlem with his daughter and grandkids. On his way out, a counselor handed him a voter registration form. He mailed it in. He’s voted in every election since. His older brother Mike, released in 2018, was told he couldn’t vote on parole — and gave up. The law changed in 2021.

THE 2021 LAW

In New York, voting rights are restored the moment you’re released from prison — parole, probation, post-release all eligible. But you must re-register after a prison sentence — the law requires it. Your old registration was likely canceled while you were inside. Misdemeanors never disqualify. In jail awaiting trial? You can vote absentee.

FOR CARL
Check your status at voterlookup.elections.ny.gov — then re-register today to make sure you’re on the rolls. Everyone except those currently in prison for a felony can vote in NY — on parole, on probation, with a federal or out-of-state record. Spread the word: too many people still believe the old rules.
