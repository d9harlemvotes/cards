NY Gender Recognition Act

Jeannine’s Protection

18, in foster care, registering for the first time. Her ID still says Jean. She has been Jeannine since middle school.

When the paperwork lags behind the person.

THE SITUATION

Jeannine Jackson is 18, lives in Harlem in foster care, and is registering to vote for the first time. She has been Jeannine — she/her — since middle school. But her NY State ID still says Jean Jackson. So does her birth certificate, held by an agency in another state.

WHAT THE SAVE ACT WOULD DO

First-time voters would need to show documentary proof of citizenship. Every document Jeannine could produce would say Jean Jackson, not Jeannine. To register, she would have to hand over her deadname* to a federal voter file.

FOR JEANNINE
New York’s Gender Recognition Act (2021) lets her update state IDs by self-attestation — no court order, no doctor’s note. To seal her name-change records, she can petition a judge. Free legal help is available in NYC.
