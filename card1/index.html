How NY state laws protect voting rights

Denise's District

Harlem, 30 years. The federal tool that protected her vote just got weaker. New York’s did not.

A federal setback — and a state backstop.

THE SITUATION

Denise has lived in Harlem for 30 years. Her congressional district was drawn so Black voters could elect the candidate of their choice — a majority-Black district. That protection traces back to the federal Voting Rights Act of 1965.

WHAT JUST CHANGED

On April 29, 2026, the Supreme Court ruled 6–3 in Louisiana v. Callais that Section 2 lawsuits now require proof of intentional discrimination — not just discriminatory results. In dissent, Justice Kagan called Section 2 “all but a dead letter.” If a future map cracks Harlem apart, federal court is now far harder.

FOR DENISE
New York’s John R. Lewis Voting Rights Act — signed on Juneteenth 2022 at Medgar Evers College — lets her sue in state court instead. Her vote is still protected. Voters in states without such laws have lost their main tool.
