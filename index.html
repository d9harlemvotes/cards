Shelter as Residence

James’s Address

Living in a shelter after losing his apartment. No lease, no utility bill — and every right to vote.

Your address is wherever you lay your head — even temporarily.

THE SITUATION

James is 47 and grew up in Harlem. After losing his apartment last year, he has been staying at a shelter on West 127th Street while he gets back on his feet. He wants to vote in November — but every registration form asks for an address, and he has no lease, no utility bill, and no permanent home of his own.

THE NEW YORK RULE

New York does not require a permanent address, a lease, or a utility bill to register. Under New York’s residence rules (N.Y. Election Law §5-104), the place you currently live — including a shelter — counts as your residence for voting. James can register using the shelter as his residence, or describe where he stays.

FOR JAMES
James can register today using the shelter as his residence. For the mailing address — where his voter card is sent — he can use the shelter, a P.O. box, or a trusted friend or relative’s address. Being without a permanent home does not take away his right to vote. Free help is available through NYC shelters and voter-registration groups.
