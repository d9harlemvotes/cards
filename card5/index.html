Deacon Johnson Educates

A Harlem poll worker for 20 years. He’s watched the federal VRA rise and fall, and was hopeful when New York built its own.

The protection most New Yorkers don’t know they have.

THE SITUATION

Deacon Johnson is 70, and has been working the polls for the last 20 years. He has seen voter turnout decline over the decades, especially in local and state elections. He was a child when the federal Voting Rights Act was passed (1965), a young man when it was expanded (1975), middle-aged when it was gutted in Shelby County (2013), and an elder when it was weakened again in Callais (April 2026). He understands how weaker voter protections chip away at the civil rights his parents and grandparents fought for.

WHAT NEW YORK BUILT

New York’s John R. Lewis Voting Rights Act, signed on Juneteenth 2022 at Medgar Evers College in Brooklyn. The strongest piece is preclearance: in jurisdictions with histories of voting discrimination, officials must get NY Attorney General approval before moving polling places, changing hours, cutting early voting days, or redrawing local districts.

KNOW YOUR VOTING RIGHTS
The law gives voters a state-court right to sue, language access protections, and a civil remedy for voter intimidation. But protection only works when people know it exists. Deacon Johnson educates his intergenerational congregation, helping them understand the laws that protect them and empowering more of them to vote.
