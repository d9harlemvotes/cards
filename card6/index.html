Mail or drop off

Renée's Trip

Out of town the week of November 3. Mailed her ballot two weeks early — while the postmark rule still counts.

The postmark window — and how to stay inside it.

THE SITUATION

Renée is 42, lives in Hamilton Heights, produces corporate events. Her biggest job of the year — a three-day conference in Atlanta — runs the week of November 3. She won’t be at her Harlem poll site on Election Day.

THE POSTMARK RULE

In New York right now, your ballot counts if it’s postmarked by Election Day and received within seven days. But in March 2026 the Supreme Court heard Watson v. RNC — a case that could end that grace period for federal races. A decision is expected by June. And a December 2025 USPS rule means mail isn’t always postmarked the day it’s dropped in the box.

FOR RENÉE
She mailed her ballot two weeks before Election Day — well ahead of any USPS or postmark surprise. Even safer: drop your sealed ballot at any poll site, early voting site, or Board of Elections office. No stamp, no postmark, no risk.
