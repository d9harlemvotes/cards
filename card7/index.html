The safety net

Tasha's Move

Brooklyn to Harlem this spring. Forgot to update her registration — so she asked for an affidavit ballot.

If your name’s not on the list, you still have the right to vote.

THE SITUATION

Tasha is 31. After college she shared a Bed-Stuy walk-up with a roommate. This spring she signed her first solo lease — a small one-bedroom in Harlem. Between the move and starting back at work, updating her voter registration slipped. On Election Day, her name isn’t on the list at her new poll site.

THE AFFIDAVIT BALLOT

New York law is clear: if you moved within the state at least 30 days before Election Day, you can still vote at your new poll site — by affidavit ballot. The envelope you seal it in doubles as a registration update. And NYC is one Board of Elections — a cross-borough move doesn’t change that.

FOR TASHA
Poll workers must offer you an affidavit ballot — they can’t refuse, and they can’t make you wait while they call the Board of Elections. Also covered: inactive status, missing signature, first-time voter ID issues, or being told you already voted when you didn’t.
