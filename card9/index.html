Teens 16 & 17 can pre-register to vote

Priya & Preston

Same table, two forms. One signs up to vote this year — the other is locked in two years early.

Two siblings, two timelines — and neither one waited.

THE SITUATION

Priya is 16, a junior. Her brother Preston is 17, a senior — and his birthday lands right on Election Day. At a registration table outside school, they fill out two different forms. Priya can’t vote yet. Preston can.

THE NEW YORK RULE

In New York you can pre-register at 16 or 17. Your registration switches on automatically the day you turn 18 — no second form, no new deadline. And if you’ll be 18 by Election Day, like Preston, you can register and vote in that election as a 17-year-old right now.

FOR PRIYA & PRESTON
Preston votes this year. Priya’s pre-registration is already locked in and waiting. The earlier you’re in the system, the harder it is for a missed deadline or a paperwork problem to keep you out.
